# ACC/AHA Guideline: Management of Blood Cholesterol

## Source
Grundy SM, et al. 2018 ACC/AHA Guideline on the Management of Blood Cholesterol. Circulation. 2019;139(25).

## Lipid Targets
- LDL-C is the primary target of therapy
- Very high risk (ASCVD + multiple risk factors): LDL-C < 70 mg/dL, consider < 55 mg/dL
- High risk (clinical ASCVD): LDL-C reduction ≥ 50% with high-intensity statin
- Moderate risk (diabetes, age 40–75): moderate-to-high intensity statin
- Triglycerides ≥ 150 mg/dL: address lifestyle factors first, consider fibrate or omega-3 if ≥ 500

## Statin Intensity
- High intensity: atorvastatin 40–80 mg, rosuvastatin 20–40 mg (LDL reduction ≥ 50%)
- Moderate intensity: atorvastatin 10–20 mg, rosuvastatin 5–10 mg, simvastatin 20–40 mg (LDL reduction 30–49%)
- Low intensity: simvastatin 10 mg, pravastatin 10–20 mg (LDL reduction < 30%)

## Non-HDL Cholesterol
- Non-HDL = total cholesterol minus HDL
- Target: LDL target + 30 mg/dL
- Better predictor of cardiovascular risk than LDL alone in patients with high triglycerides

## Monitoring
- Fasting lipid panel 4–12 weeks after starting or adjusting statin therapy
- Then every 3–12 months as needed
- Check ALT before starting statin; repeat if symptoms of hepatotoxicity
- CK only if symptomatic (muscle pain, weakness)

## Lifestyle Modifications
- Heart-healthy diet: reduce saturated fat to < 6% of calories
- Regular aerobic exercise: 150 minutes/week moderate intensity
- Weight management: 5–10% weight loss improves lipid profile
- Limit alcohol, eliminate trans fats

## Special Populations
- Diabetes: moderate-to-high intensity statin for all patients aged 40–75
- CKD: statin therapy recommended for stage 3–5 CKD (not on dialysis)
- Elderly (> 75): continue statin if already tolerating; individualize new starts
- Statin intolerance: try different statin, lower dose, or every-other-day dosing before adding non-statin

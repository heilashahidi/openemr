# GOLD Report: COPD Management

## Source
Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD). Global Strategy for the Diagnosis, Management, and Prevention of COPD. 2026 Report.

## Diagnosis
- Spirometry required: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70 confirms airflow limitation
- GOLD staging by FEV1 (% predicted): I mild (≥80), II moderate (50–79), III severe (30–49), IV very severe (<30)
- Assess symptoms with CAT score or mMRC dyspnea scale

## Pharmacotherapy Stepwise Approach
- Group A (few symptoms, low risk): short-acting bronchodilator PRN
- Group B (more symptoms, low risk): long-acting bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA)
- Group E (exacerbations): LABA + LAMA; add ICS if eosinophils ≥ 300
- Triple therapy (LABA + LAMA + ICS) for persistent exacerbations despite dual therapy

## Exacerbation Management
- Increased dyspnea, sputum volume, or sputum purulence beyond normal day-to-day variation
- Mild: increase bronchodilator use
- Moderate: add oral corticosteroids (prednisone 40 mg × 5 days) and/or antibiotics
- Severe: emergency department or hospitalization
- Antibiotics if purulent sputum: amoxicillin/clavulanate, azithromycin, or doxycycline

## Monitoring
- Spirometry annually to track FEV1 decline
- Pulse oximetry at each visit; ABG if SpO2 < 92%
- Screen for comorbidities: cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis, depression, lung cancer
- Annual flu vaccine; pneumococcal and COVID vaccines per guidelines

## Oxygen Therapy
- Long-term oxygen if PaO2 ≤ 55 mmHg or SpO2 ≤ 88% at rest
- Improves survival in hypoxemic COPD
- Goal SpO2: 88–92% (avoid over-oxygenation in CO2 retainers)

## Worsening Shortness of Breath Workup
- Differentiate COPD exacerbation from: pneumonia, PE, heart failure, pneumothorax
- BNP or NT-proBNP to evaluate for concurrent heart failure
- Chest X-ray to rule out pneumonia or other pathology
- D-dimer or CT-PA if PE suspected

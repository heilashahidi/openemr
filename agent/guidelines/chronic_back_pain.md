# ACP Guideline: Chronic Low Back Pain

## Source
Qaseem A, et al. Noninvasive Treatments for Acute, Subacute, and Chronic Low Back Pain. Ann Intern Med. 2017;166(7).

## Classification
- Acute: < 4 weeks
- Subacute: 4–12 weeks
- Chronic: > 12 weeks
- Red flags requiring urgent evaluation: saddle anesthesia, progressive neurological deficit, bowel/bladder dysfunction, history of cancer, unexplained weight loss, fever

## First-Line Non-Pharmacologic
- Exercise: most strongly recommended — core strengthening, aerobic, stretching
- Physical therapy: structured program with progressive loading
- Heat therapy for acute episodes
- Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR)
- Spinal manipulation, acupuncture, massage — moderate evidence

## Pharmacotherapy
- NSAIDs: first-line pharmacologic for acute and chronic LBP
- Muscle relaxants: short-term only for acute LBP with spasm (cyclobenzaprine, tizanidine)
- Duloxetine 60 mg: recommended for chronic LBP, especially with comorbid depression
- Acetaminophen: NOT recommended (no better than placebo for LBP)
- Opioids: last resort only, short-term, after all other options exhausted

## Exercise Limitations
- Back pain flares may temporarily limit exercise capacity
- Modify, don't stop: switch from high-impact to low-impact activities
- Walking, swimming, recumbent cycling during flares
- Physical therapy referral if unable to exercise independently
- Goal: return to full activity as soon as tolerated

## Imaging
- NOT recommended in first 6 weeks unless red flags present
- MRI for suspected radiculopathy, spinal stenosis, or if surgery being considered
- Incidental imaging findings (disc bulging, degenerative changes) are common in asymptomatic patients

## When to Refer
- Persistent symptoms > 12 weeks despite conservative treatment
- Progressive neurological deficit
- Suspected spinal stenosis with functional limitation
- Consideration of interventional procedures (epidural steroid injection, nerve block)

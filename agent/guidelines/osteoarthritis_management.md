# ACR/AF Osteoarthritis Management Guidelines

## Source
Kolasinski SL, et al. 2019 ACR/AF Guideline for Management of Osteoarthritis of the Hand, Hip, and Knee. Arthritis Care Res. 2020;72(2).

## Diagnosis
- Clinical diagnosis: joint pain worsening with activity, stiffness < 30 minutes, crepitus, bony enlargement
- Imaging not required for diagnosis but may confirm: joint space narrowing, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis
- Labs normal (RF negative, ESR/CRP normal) — helps distinguish from inflammatory arthritis

## First-Line Treatment
- Exercise: strongly recommended — land-based, aquatic, tai chi, yoga
- Weight management: 5–10% weight loss significantly reduces knee pain
- Physical therapy: structured exercise program with strengthening and flexibility
- Topical NSAIDs: diclofenac gel — preferred over oral for knee/hand OA, especially age > 75

## Pharmacotherapy
- Oral NSAIDs: ibuprofen, naproxen, celecoxib — effective but use lowest dose for shortest duration
- Acetaminophen: conditionally NOT recommended as monotherapy (limited efficacy)
- Duloxetine 60 mg: conditionally recommended for knee OA, especially if comorbid depression
- Intra-articular corticosteroids: short-term relief (4–8 weeks); limit to 3–4 injections/year per joint

## Avoid or Use with Caution
- Opioids: strongly NOT recommended for chronic OA pain
- Oral corticosteroids: NOT recommended
- Glucosamine/chondroitin: conditionally NOT recommended (insufficient evidence)
- Hyaluronic acid injections: conditionally NOT recommended for knee OA

## Monitoring
- Pain and function assessment at each visit (WOMAC or similar)
- GI and renal monitoring if on chronic NSAIDs
- Blood pressure monitoring (NSAIDs can raise BP)
- Fall risk assessment in elderly with lower extremity OA

## Surgical Referral
- Consider total joint replacement if: persistent pain despite conservative treatment, significant functional limitation, imaging evidence of advanced disease
- Best outcomes when BMI < 40 and no active infection

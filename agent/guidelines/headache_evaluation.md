# AAN Guideline: Headache Evaluation in Primary Care

## Source
American Academy of Neurology. Practice Guideline Update: Evaluation of Headache in Adults. Neurology. 2024;102(5).

## New-Onset Headache Workup
- Detailed headache history: onset, duration, frequency, location, quality, triggers, associated symptoms
- Neurological examination: cranial nerves, motor, sensory, reflexes, fundoscopy
- Blood pressure measurement (hypertensive headache)
- Basic labs: CBC, BMP, ESR/CRP if temporal arteritis suspected

## Red Flags (SNOOP)
- S: Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss) or secondary risk factors (HIV, cancer)
- N: Neurological symptoms or signs (confusion, weakness, vision changes)
- O: Onset sudden (thunderclap headache — rule out SAH)
- O: Older age of onset (> 50 — consider temporal arteritis)
- P: Pattern change (progressive worsening, positional, worst headache of life)

## When to Image
- New headache with any red flag → MRI brain with or without contrast
- Thunderclap headache → CT head non-contrast urgently, then LP if CT negative
- Chronic headache without red flags → imaging NOT routinely recommended
- Headache with papilledema → urgent MRI + MRV

## Headaches Lasting Weeks to Months
- New daily persistent headache: abrupt onset of daily headache in a person without prior headache history
- Medication overuse headache: suspect if using acute medications > 10–15 days/month
- Tension-type headache: bilateral, pressing/tightening, mild-moderate, not aggravated by activity
- Consider: depression, anxiety, sleep disorders, TMJ dysfunction as contributing factors

## Primary Headache Syndromes
- Migraine: episodic, unilateral, pulsating, 4–72 hours, nausea, photo/phonophobia
- Tension-type: bilateral, pressing, 30 min–7 days, no nausea
- Cluster: severe unilateral periorbital, 15–180 min, autonomic features (tearing, rhinorrhea)

## Initial Management for New Headache
- Headache diary for 4 weeks: frequency, severity, triggers, medications used
- Trial of acute treatment (NSAIDs for mild, triptans for suspected migraine)
- Follow-up in 4–6 weeks to reassess
- If persistent or worsening, refer to neurology

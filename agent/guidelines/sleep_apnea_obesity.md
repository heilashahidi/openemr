# AASM/ACP Guidelines: Obstructive Sleep Apnea and Obesity Management

## Source
Patil SP, et al. Treatment of Adult OSA with Positive Airway Pressure. J Clin Sleep Med. 2024;20(5).
Garvey WT, et al. AGA Clinical Practice Guideline on Pharmacological Interventions for Adults with Obesity. Gastroenterology. 2024;167(4).

## OSA Diagnosis
- Polysomnography (PSG) is gold standard; home sleep apnea test (HSAT) acceptable for moderate-high pretest probability
- AHI (apnea-hypopnea index): mild 5–14, moderate 15–29, severe ≥ 30
- Symptoms: snoring, witnessed apneas, excessive daytime sleepiness, morning headaches, nocturia

## CPAP Therapy
- First-line for moderate-severe OSA
- Target usage: ≥ 4 hours/night on ≥ 70% of nights
- Auto-titrating CPAP (APAP) acceptable as initial therapy
- Follow-up at 1 month for adherence and efficacy; then every 6–12 months
- Benefits: reduces daytime sleepiness, improves BP, reduces cardiovascular events in adherent patients
- If CPAP intolerant: oral appliance, positional therapy, or surgical options

## Cardiovascular Impact of OSA
- Untreated OSA increases risk: hypertension, atrial fibrillation, heart failure, stroke
- CPAP reduces blood pressure by 2–5 mmHg in adherent patients
- OSA screening recommended in patients with resistant hypertension, AFib, or HFrEF
- Optimizing CPAP may improve heart failure outcomes and reduce AFib burden

## Obesity Assessment
- BMI 25–29.9: overweight; 30–34.9: class I obesity; 35–39.9: class II; ≥ 40: class III
- Waist circumference: > 40 inches (men), > 35 inches (women) indicates central obesity
- Assess for obesity-related comorbidities: T2DM, HTN, dyslipidemia, NAFLD, OSA, OA

## Obesity Management
- Lifestyle intervention: 500–750 kcal/day deficit, 150+ min/week moderate exercise
- Target: 5–10% weight loss in 6 months — clinically meaningful improvement in comorbidities
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly): 15–17% weight loss, cardiovascular benefit
- Tirzepatide: 20–25% weight loss in clinical trials
- Bariatric surgery: consider if BMI ≥ 40 or BMI ≥ 35 with obesity-related comorbidities

## Weight and Diabetes
- 5% weight loss improves HbA1c by 0.5–1.0%
- 10% weight loss may achieve diabetes remission in early disease
- GLP-1 RA and SGLT2 inhibitors promote weight loss while improving glycemic control
- Avoid weight-gaining medications when possible (sulfonylureas, insulin, TZDs)

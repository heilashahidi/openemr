# ACG Clinical Guideline: Irritable Bowel Syndrome

## Source
Lacy BE, et al. ACG Clinical Guideline: Management of Irritable Bowel Syndrome. Am J Gastroenterol. 2024;119(1).

## Diagnosis
- Rome IV criteria: recurrent abdominal pain ≥ 1 day/week in last 3 months, associated with ≥ 2 of: related to defecation, change in stool frequency, change in stool form
- Subtypes: IBS-C (constipation), IBS-D (diarrhea), IBS-M (mixed)
- Limited testing recommended: CBC, CRP, celiac serology; colonoscopy only if alarm features

## Alarm Features Requiring Further Workup
- Age > 50 without prior colon cancer screening
- Rectal bleeding, unintentional weight loss, nocturnal symptoms
- Family history of colorectal cancer or IBD
- Anemia, elevated inflammatory markers

## Dietary Management
- Low FODMAP diet: strongest evidence for symptom reduction (70% response rate)
- Implement with dietitian guidance; eliminate for 2–6 weeks then reintroduce
- Fiber: soluble fiber (psyllium) beneficial; insoluble fiber may worsen symptoms
- Avoid trigger foods: caffeine, alcohol, fatty foods, artificial sweeteners

## Pharmacotherapy by Subtype
- IBS-C: linaclotide, lubiprostone, PEG
- IBS-D: loperamide PRN, rifaximin 550 mg TID × 14 days, eluxadoline
- Pain-predominant: antispasmodics (dicyclomine, hyoscyamine), low-dose TCA (amitriptyline 10–25 mg), neuromodulators

## Bloating and Gas
- Bloating is the most bothersome symptom for many patients
- Low FODMAP diet most effective for bloating
- Simethicone, peppermint oil capsules may help
- Rifaximin can reduce bloating in IBS-D

## Psychological Therapies
- CBT and gut-directed hypnotherapy have strong evidence
- Consider for refractory symptoms or significant psychological comorbidity
- Depression and anxiety co-occur in 40–60% of IBS patients
- SSRIs may help IBS-C; TCAs may help IBS-D

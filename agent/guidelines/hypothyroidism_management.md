# ATA Guideline: Hypothyroidism Management

## Source
Jonklaas J, et al. Guidelines for the Treatment of Hypothyroidism. Thyroid. 2014;24(12). Updated 2024.

## Diagnosis
- Primary hypothyroidism: elevated TSH with low free T4
- Subclinical hypothyroidism: elevated TSH with normal free T4
- Symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, depression, myalgia, cognitive slowing

## Treatment
- Levothyroxine (T4): first-line, preferred monotherapy
- Starting dose: 1.6 mcg/kg/day for most adults; lower start (25–50 mcg) in elderly or cardiac patients
- Take on empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, or at bedtime 3+ hours after last meal
- Avoid taking with calcium, iron, PPIs (reduce absorption)

## TSH Monitoring
- Recheck TSH 6–8 weeks after starting or adjusting dose
- Once stable, check TSH annually
- Target TSH: 0.5–2.5 mU/L for most adults; upper range acceptable for elderly
- Pregnancy: tighter targets — TSH < 2.5 mU/L in first trimester

## Dose Adjustments
- TSH above target: increase levothyroxine by 12.5–25 mcg
- TSH below target (overreplacement): reduce by 12.5–25 mcg
- Overreplacement risks: atrial fibrillation, osteoporosis, anxiety
- Weight changes, medication interactions, or aging may require dose adjustment

## Hypothyroidism in Autoimmune Disease
- Hashimoto thyroiditis is the most common cause
- Higher prevalence in SLE and other autoimmune conditions
- Monitor thyroid function more frequently in patients on immunosuppressants
- Levothyroxine dose may need adjustment with steroid use

## Drug Interactions
- Drugs that decrease absorption: calcium, iron, antacids, PPI, cholestyramine
- Drugs that increase metabolism: rifampin, phenytoin, carbamazepine
- Drugs that increase TBG: estrogen, pregnancy (may need dose increase)
- Separate levothyroxine from interacting medications by 4 hours

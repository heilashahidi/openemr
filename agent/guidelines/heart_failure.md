# ACC/AHA Heart Failure Management Guidelines

## Source
Heidenreich PA, et al. 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure. Circulation. 2022;145(18).

## Classification
- HFrEF: heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (EF ≤ 40%)
- HFmrEF: heart failure with mildly reduced EF (41–49%)
- HFpEF: heart failure with preserved EF (≥ 50%)
- NYHA Class I–IV based on symptom severity

## Core Medications for HFrEF (Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy)
- ACE inhibitor or ARB (or ARNI: sacubitril/valsartan preferred if tolerated)
- Beta-blocker: carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or bisoprolol
- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA): spironolactone or eplerenone
- SGLT2 inhibitor: dapagliflozin or empagliflozin (regardless of diabetes status)
- All four medication classes should be initiated and titrated to target doses

## Diuretics
- Loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide) for volume management
- Not disease-modifying — used for symptom relief
- Titrate to achieve euvolemia; monitor daily weights
- Signs of volume overload: peripheral edema, weight gain > 2 lbs/day or 5 lbs/week, dyspnea, JVD

## BNP and NT-proBNP
- BNP > 100 pg/mL or NT-proBNP > 300 pg/mL suggests heart failure
- BNP > 400 pg/mL: high probability of HF as cause of symptoms
- Serial monitoring useful for tracking treatment response
- Rising BNP may indicate decompensation before symptoms appear

## Monitoring
- Renal function and electrolytes within 1–2 weeks of starting ACE/ARB/MRA
- Monitor potassium closely with MRA (risk of hyperkalemia)
- Echocardiogram at baseline and to reassess EF after optimization
- Weight monitoring: patient should weigh daily and report gain > 2 lbs/day

## Volume Management
- Fluid restriction: typically 1.5–2 L/day in advanced HF
- Sodium restriction: < 2 g/day
- Weight gain of 5 lbs in one week or 2 lbs in one day should prompt evaluation for decompensation
- Adjust diuretic dose accordingly; do not wait for symptoms to worsen

## Anticoagulation in HF with AFib
- CHA2DS2-VASc score guides anticoagulation decision
- Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) preferred over warfarin: apixaban, rivaroxaban
- Dual antiplatelet + anticoagulant increases bleeding risk; minimize duration

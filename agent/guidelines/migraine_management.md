# AHS Migraine Management Guidelines

## Source
American Headache Society. Consensus Statement on Migraine Prevention and Acute Treatment. Headache. 2025;65(1).

## Diagnosis
- Migraine without aura: ≥ 5 attacks lasting 4–72 hours with at least 2 of: unilateral, pulsating, moderate-severe, aggravated by activity; plus nausea/vomiting or photophobia/phonophobia
- Chronic migraine: ≥ 15 headache days/month for > 3 months, with migraine features on ≥ 8 days

## Acute Treatment
- Triptans: sumatriptan 50–100 mg, rizatriptan 10 mg — first-line for moderate-severe
- NSAIDs: ibuprofen 400–600 mg, naproxen 500 mg — for mild-moderate
- Limit acute medication to < 10 days/month to prevent medication overuse headache
- Treat early: most effective within 1 hour of onset
- Antiemetics (metoclopramide, ondansetron) for associated nausea

## Prevention Criteria
- Consider preventive therapy if: ≥ 4 migraine days/month, significant disability, acute treatment failure, or medication overuse
- Goal: reduce frequency by ≥ 50%

## Preventive Medications
- First-line oral: topiramate 50–100 mg/day, propranolol 80–240 mg/day, amitriptyline 25–75 mg/day
- CGRP monoclonal antibodies: erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab — for patients who fail 2+ oral preventives
- OnabotulinumtoxinA: FDA-approved for chronic migraine (≥ 15 days/month)
- Adequate trial: 8–12 weeks at target dose before switching

## Frequency Monitoring
- Headache diary: track days, severity, triggers, acute medication use
- Increasing frequency (e.g., 4–5 per month) warrants preventive therapy optimization
- If frequency increases despite prevention, evaluate for medication overuse or new secondary cause

## Lifestyle Modifications
- Regular sleep schedule, adequate hydration, regular meals
- Identify and avoid triggers (stress, certain foods, alcohol, hormonal changes)
- Regular aerobic exercise (150 min/week) reduces frequency
- Behavioral therapy: CBT, biofeedback, relaxation training

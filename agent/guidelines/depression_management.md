# APA Practice Guideline: Major Depressive Disorder

## Source
American Psychiatric Association. Practice Guideline for the Treatment of MDD, 3rd Edition. 2023.

## Screening and Diagnosis
- PHQ-9 score 5–9: mild; 10–14: moderate; 15–19: moderately severe; 20+: severe
- Diagnosis requires ≥ 5 symptoms for ≥ 2 weeks including depressed mood or anhedonia
- Rule out medical causes: thyroid disease, anemia, vitamin D deficiency, medication side effects
- Screen for bipolar disorder before starting antidepressants (MDQ or similar)

## First-Line Pharmacotherapy
- SSRIs: sertraline, escitalopram, fluoxetine (best evidence base, fewest interactions)
- SNRIs: duloxetine, venlafaxine (consider if comorbid pain or neuropathy)
- Allow 4–6 weeks at adequate dose before concluding inadequate response
- Start low, titrate based on response and tolerability

## Treatment Response
- Full response: ≥ 50% reduction in PHQ-9 from baseline
- Partial response at 4–6 weeks: optimize dose before switching
- No response at 6–8 weeks: switch to different class or augment
- Remission goal: PHQ-9 < 5

## Duration of Treatment
- First episode: continue for 6–12 months after remission
- Recurrent episodes (2+): consider long-term maintenance therapy
- Taper gradually over 2–4 weeks to avoid discontinuation syndrome
- Never stop abruptly, especially paroxetine and venlafaxine

## Insomnia and Depression
- Sleep disturbance present in 75% of depressed patients
- Treat insomnia concurrently — improves depression outcomes
- Trazodone 25–100 mg at bedtime as adjunct for sleep
- CBT-I (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia) is first-line for chronic insomnia
- Avoid benzodiazepines for long-term use

## Comorbid Anxiety
- GAD and MDD co-occur in 50–60% of cases
- SSRIs and SNRIs treat both conditions
- Duloxetine effective for depression, anxiety, and chronic pain
- Buspirone as augmentation for persistent anxiety

## Monitoring
- Follow-up within 1–2 weeks of starting or changing medication
- PHQ-9 at every visit to track response
- Monitor for suicidal ideation, especially in first 4 weeks
- Assess for medication side effects: weight gain, sexual dysfunction, GI symptoms

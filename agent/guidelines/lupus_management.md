# ACR/EULAR Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Management

## Source
Fanouriakis A, et al. 2023 EULAR recommendations for the management of SLE. Ann Rheum Dis. 2024;83(1).

## Diagnosis and Monitoring
- ANA positive in > 95% of SLE patients
- Anti-dsDNA antibodies correlate with disease activity and lupus nephritis
- Complement levels (C3, C4): low levels suggest active disease
- Monitor CBC, CMP, urinalysis, anti-dsDNA, and complement every 3–6 months

## Flare Recognition
- Joint pain, fatigue, malar rash (butterfly rash), photosensitivity
- New or worsening proteinuria suggests renal flare
- Falling complement + rising anti-dsDNA = classic flare pattern
- Weight changes, fever, oral ulcers, hair loss may indicate active disease

## Core Medications
- Hydroxychloroquine: ALL SLE patients unless contraindicated; reduces flares, organ damage, and mortality
- Target dose: 5 mg/kg actual body weight (reduces retinal toxicity risk)
- Annual ophthalmologic screening after 5 years of use

## Flare Management
- Mild (skin, joints): NSAIDs (short-term), topical steroids, hydroxychloroquine dose optimization
- Moderate: low-dose prednisone (≤ 7.5 mg/day), add immunosuppressant
- Severe (nephritis, CNS): high-dose steroids + mycophenolate or cyclophosphamide
- Goal: minimize chronic steroid use to ≤ 7.5 mg/day prednisone equivalent

## Lupus Nephritis
- Class III/IV (proliferative): induction with mycophenolate mofetil or cyclophosphamide + steroids
- Maintenance: mycophenolate preferred over azathioprine
- Monitor: urinalysis, UACR, serum creatinine, eGFR every 1–3 months during active nephritis
- Belimumab or voclosporin as add-on for refractory disease

## Cardiovascular Risk in SLE
- SLE patients have 2–3x increased cardiovascular risk
- Aggressive management of traditional risk factors (HTN, hyperlipidemia, diabetes)
- Hydroxychloroquine has cardioprotective effects
- Minimize steroid exposure (increases metabolic syndrome risk)

## Depression and Fatigue in SLE
- Depression prevalence 25–40% in SLE patients
- Fatigue is the most common and debilitating symptom
- Screen with PHQ-9 at regular intervals
- Duloxetine may address both depression and musculoskeletal pain
- Exercise programs improve fatigue when disease is stable

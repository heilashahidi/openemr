# Anticoagulation Management in Atrial Fibrillation

## Source
January CT, et al. 2019 AHA/ACC/HRS Focused Update of the 2014 Guideline for Management of AF. Circulation. 2019;140(2).

## CHA2DS2-VASc Score
- C: CHF (1 point)
- H: Hypertension (1 point)
- A2: Age ≥ 75 (2 points)
- D: Diabetes (1 point)
- S2: Stroke/TIA/thromboembolism (2 points)
- V: Vascular disease (1 point)
- A: Age 65–74 (1 point)
- Sc: Sex category — female (1 point)
- Score ≥ 2 (men) or ≥ 3 (women): anticoagulation recommended

## Preferred Anticoagulants
- DOACs preferred over warfarin for non-valvular AF
- Apixaban 5 mg BID (dose reduce to 2.5 mg BID if ≥ 2 of: age ≥ 80, weight ≤ 60 kg, creatinine ≥ 1.5)
- Rivaroxaban 20 mg daily with evening meal (reduce to 15 mg if CrCl 15–50)
- Dabigatran 150 mg BID (reduce to 75 mg BID if CrCl 15–30)
- Warfarin: target INR 2.0–3.0; requires regular monitoring

## Dual Therapy Considerations
- Aspirin + anticoagulant increases bleeding risk significantly
- Avoid dual therapy unless compelling indication (e.g., recent coronary stent)
- If dual therapy required, limit duration and prefer low-dose aspirin (81 mg)
- Triple therapy (aspirin + P2Y12 + anticoagulant) should be minimized to ≤ 1 week if possible

## Bleeding Risk Assessment
- HAS-BLED score identifies modifiable bleeding risk factors
- High risk does NOT mean withhold anticoagulation — address modifiable factors
- Modifiable factors: uncontrolled HTN, labile INR, concomitant NSAIDs/antiplatelet, excess alcohol

## Renal Dosing
- Monitor renal function at least annually on DOACs
- CrCl < 15: avoid all DOACs; consider warfarin
- CrCl 15–30: apixaban may be used with caution
- CrCl 30–50: dose-adjust rivaroxaban and dabigatran

## Periprocedural Management
- Low bleeding risk procedures: continue anticoagulation
- High bleeding risk: hold DOAC 48–72 hours before procedure
- Bridging with heparin generally NOT recommended for DOACs
- Resume 24–48 hours post-procedure depending on hemostasis
